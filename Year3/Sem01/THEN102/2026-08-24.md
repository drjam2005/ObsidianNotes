#### Christian Morality is **Involving Christ**
---
Moral Theology concerns the way Christians live in the light of what they profess about God, and at the heart of the relationship between Christian truth and human life, is "the mystery of Christ."

##### The Christian moral life is **not** separated from Christian faith.
- What Christians believe about Christ, shapes how they understand freedom, happiness, human dignity, conscience, relationships, and moral responsibility

#### The Problem:
- Morality is Reduced to External Rule-Following
    - Do not lie.
    - Do not steal.
    - Do not harm others.
    - ...
    > The commandments are not just walls that stop us from doing wrong. They are **guides** that help us grow in love and become the kind of people God calls us to be.

    > Jesus teaches the commandments not as the minimum requirement, but as a path that changes the heart and leads us toward a deeper, more loving way of life.

- Morality is Reduced to Personal Preference
    > The opposite error is to treat personal experience, feelings, or preferences as the final standard of morality. Personal experience is important because moral decisions occur in concrete situations, but experience must be examined and interpreted. It cannot automatically determine what is true or good.

    > Grabowski warns that experience should not be used to override Scripture, Tradition, moral norms, or reason. Human experience is also wounded and distorted by sin. Authentic Christian experience is the fruit of an encounter with Christ.

###### Is something morally good, simply because I feel it is right, or must my experience be examined in the light of Christ and the truth of the Gospel?


### Christ as the Fulfillment of the Law
- Jesus Does not Abolish the Law
    > "Christ is the key to Scripture and the center of salvation history. He does not reject the commandments; he brings them to their fullness."
- From External Action to the Heart
    - "You shall not murder"; becomes a call to attentive love that protects life.
    - The prohibition against adultery becomes a call to purity of heart and respect for the dignity of the other person.
    - Moral life is not only about what is externally visible; it also involves desires, intentions, attitudes, and the way one sees others.

    Jesus therefore moves moral reflection from the question:
    > "What is the minimum I must do?

    To the question:

    > "How is Christ calling me to love?"

##### Love is the opposite of Sin

### The Commandments as a Path
- **Emphasize three points:**
    - The commandments protect the good of the person and the community.
    - The commandments educate the person in love.
    - The commandments lead toward a deeper relationship with Christ.

    > The commandments are not opposed to freedom. They guide freedom toward tits true fulfillment: the ability to love what is good.
### Christ as the Living Law
> Jesus is not only a teacher who gives moral instructions. He is the **living fulfillment** of the law. Through the gift of the Spirit, he enables believers to share in his life and love and to express that love through concrete choices and actions.

> "I am the vine, you are the branches, you cut me away; you wither in life"

###### What difference does it make if morality is about following Jesus rather than just obeying rules?
- It's all about the **intention**, if one were to just obeying rules, they don't have what it takes to be closer with the Lord

### Christocentrism and Christian Moral Theology
- Christocentrism means putting Jesus at the center of how Chritians think, decide, and live.
- It's seeing life, choices, and moral responsibility through the eyes of Jesus.
    - If you center tour life on your goals, your decisions follow your goals.
    - If you center tour life on your friends, your decisions follow your friends.
    - If you center tour life on your Christ, your decisions follow Christ.
-  Christocentrism doesn't cancel reason, experience, Scripture, or moral principles. It organizes. Ut helps us understand all these things in thel ight of Jesus and His Gospel

- Rules matter, but they make sense only because they point us to Christ. The goal is nont just to obey -- it's to become like Jesus.

- Imagine morality is a journey.
    - Rules are road signs.
    - Christ is the person walking with you.
    - Christocentrism means you follow Him, not just the signs

##### Christocentrism means living, choosing, and thinking with Jesus at the center.

### The Moral Life as Response
- Christian morality begins with God's initiative:
1. God gives life and calls the human person into relationship
2. Christ reveals God's love in a personal and concrete way.
3. The person responds through faith, freedom, love, and obedience.
4. This response becomes visible in moral decisions and relationships.

- The moral life is therefore not an attempt to earn God's love. It is a response to the love already received.

### Conscience
- What is conscience?
- What Conscience is not:
    - just a feeling
    - what I personally prefer
    - whatever I want to do
    - an excuse to justify myself
    - fear of being judged

- **Conscience is a judement of reason...**

If your firned asks you to cheat on an exam, your feelings might say:

> I'm scared to say no...  

Your conscience says:

> Cheating is wrong

Conscience is the part of you that judges tha action -- not the emotion

A good conscience needs truth. You cannot judge well if y ou don't know what is true.

A well-informed conscience needs:
- facts -- What is really happening?
- moral principles -- What does the Church teach?
- effects - Who will be affected?
- respect for people -- Does this honor their diginity?
- ...

A sincere decision is not always a correct decision. You can be sincere and still be wrong.

### Conscience Freedom Responsibility
- Conscience helps you take responsibility for your choices -- past, present, and future.
    - When you choose well -> conscience gives peace.
    - When you choose wrongly -> conscience invites you to face the truth, ask forgiveness, ...

- Conscience is not only a judge -- it is also a place of healing

Ontological Argument (Reason Alone)
===
### The Core idea:
- One does not really need to physically see if God exists, one can prove it by using logic and definition alone
### The Main Logic (as proposed by St. Anselm):
  1. By definition, **God is "that than which nothing greater can be conceived"**, (the greatest thing one can possible imagine.
  2. Even an atheist can grasp this definition -> meaning God exists as an idea in their understanding
  3. it is **greater to exist in real life** than to exist only as an idea in someone's head.
  4. Therefore, if the greatest imaginable being only existed as an idea;
  -> we could easily imagine something even greater: that same being existing in real life.
  5. To truly be the greatest imaginable being, **God must exist in reality**.

### One of the Big Critiques:
  1. The Fallacy of Reification:
    - Knowing what a word means does not necessarily mean of it's existence
      -> Like knowing what a "unicorn" is, does **NOT** imply it's existence
  2. The Perfect Island (Gaunilo's Parody):
    - An 11th-century monk argued that by using Anselm's logic, you could prove that the "perfect island" must exist.
      -> If such perfect island can be imagined, then a far, greater island must exist


### Summing Up
- Anselm's argument is a classic example of a **rationalist argument**. His argument attemts to show that we can establish a substantial conclusion - God's existence - by reason alone. This then contradicts the empiricist principle (associated with philosophers such as Locke, Berkeley and Hume) that reason alone can never produce knowledge of reality, such reality relies essentially on the input from one or more of the five senses.
- The Ontological Argument is ingenious. It attempts to prove the existence of God merely from the idea or definition of God, which is bogus.

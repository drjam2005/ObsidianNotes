Cosmological Argument
===
### The Core idea:
- The universe exists (as we are here), and because **things don't just appear out of nowhere**, there must be something that caused it to exist
### The Logic:
1. Every single thing in the universe is **contingent** - meaning it didn't have to exist, and its existence depends on other things.
2. Since the universe is just a collection of these such things, **the universe itself must be contingent**.
3. Contingent things need a cause outside of themselves to exist.
4. Therefore:
  -> The universe must have some ultimate cause outside of space and time, such ultimate cause must be a necessary being.

### The Big Critiques:
1. The Mother Analogy (Bertrand Russel):
    - Just because every individual human as a mother, does not mean the entire human race has one collective mother.
        -> It is incorrect to assume the *whole universe* must have a cause just because *parts* inside it do.
2. An Endless Past:
    - Why can't the universe have existed forever without a beginning?
        -> (Philosopher Wittgenstein illustrated how hard it is to imagine an endless past with a joke:
        "...three, two, one, zero. Phew! I just finished counting down all the numbers from infinity." It feels conceptually impossible to "finish" a task that had no starting point.
3. Uncaused Beginnings:
    - Some modern physicists suggest the universe simply... just started without a cause, but critics counter that with "something cannot come from nothing".

### Summing Up
- There are three possible accounts of the origin of the universe: it was created by a necessary being; it has always existed; it has not alweays existed, but came from nothing. As we have seen, a defender of the Cosmological Argument can make a good case for its weakest link, by pointing out the difficulties with the two alternative proposales. In which case, the Cosmological Argument constitutes a plausible argument for God's existence.
